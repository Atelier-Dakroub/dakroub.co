I started on a machine that booted to a prompt and did nothing until you typed at it, and the habit stuck. Everything I have built since has been an attempt to make a system say what it is doing.

Professionally that has meant twenty years of production interfaces: a PHP framework at Quicken Loans that is still running, financial applications at Tradeweb, my own studio for three years, design systems for Netflix internal tooling and Apple Music at DockYard, the Kajabi Design System, and now React Native and Next.js at Heyo with Claude Code in the loop every day.

Alongside all of it, one thread has never stopped: twenty-five years of design and development for the concert saxophonist Timothy McAllister. Same collaborator, same brief, and every era of the web on this page in between.

Design and engineering have never been two jobs to me. A layout is an argument about hierarchy, a component API is an argument about vocabulary, and both of them get decided by the same person in the same afternoon or they get decided badly. The titles have moved around that idea, software engineer, front-end developer, business owner, UIX architect, UX developer, full stack engineer, and the work has stayed put.

The other half of what I do is writing, which is not an accident: the degree is a Bachelor of Fine Arts in music performance with a minor in technical writing. Documentation, design docs, the memo that settles an argument. The two things I was formally trained in are performing in front of people and explaining something in writing, and they turned out to be most of the job.
